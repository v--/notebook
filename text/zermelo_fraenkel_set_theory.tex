\section{Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory}\label{sec:zermelo_fraenkel_set_theory}

\begin{definition}\label{def:zfc}
  According to \cite[40]{Kleene1971Metamathematics}, the earliest \hyperref[def:axiomatic_set_theory]{axiomatic set theory} was described by Zermelo in his 1908 paper \cite{Zermelo2010Collection:GrundlagenDerMengenlehre}. This is the same year in which Russell published his paper \cite{Russell1908TypeTheory} introducing the \hyperref[def:ramified_theory_of_types]{ramified theory of types}, so \hyperref[def:first_order_logic]{first-order} and \hyperref[def:second_order_logic]{second-order} logic were not yet established concepts.

  Zermelo's system has since then been extensively studied and refined, and split into \enquote{true} first-order axioms and \hyperref[def:fol_adaptation_of_monadic_sol_theory]{first-order adaptations} of second-order axioms. As we shall see, several commonly stated axioms can be derived from the second-order axiom of replacement, which is itself attributed in \cite[15]{Jech2006SetTheory} to Abraham Fraenkel and Thoralf Skolem.

  We call the resulting theory \term[ru=теория множеств Цермело-Френкеля (\cite[179]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость}), en=Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory (\cite[\S 5.3.2]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof})]{Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory} and abbreviate it as \logic{ZF}. More often than not, we include the controversial axiom of choice, and abbreviate the resulting theory as \logic{ZFC}. We will call the second-order theories \( \logic{ZF}_2 \) and \( \logic{ZFC}_2 \), correspondingly.

  We begin with a minimal set of axioms, based on \cite[def. 6.1.6]{Hinman2005Logic}, which, along with the \hyperref[def:axiomatic_set_theory/extensionality]{axiom of extensionality}, we will use derive the others:
  \begin{thmenum}[series=def:zfc]
    \thmitem{def:zfc/replacement}\mimprovised The second-order \term[en=axiom of replacement (\cite[30]{Schechter1997AnalysisHandbook}), ru=аксиома подстановки (\cite[137]{КолмогоровДрагалин2006ЛогикаДополнительныеГлавы})]{axiom of replacement} allows mapping a set (\enquote{replacing its values}) according to a definable \hyperref[def:partial_function]{partial function}:
    \small
    \begin{multline}\label{eq:def:zfc/replacement/sol}
      \overbrace{ \qforall {\synp^{\syn\iota \synimplies \syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron}} \qforall {\syna^{\syn\iota}} }^{\mathclap{\substack{\synp \T*{is a binary predicate and} \\ \syna \T*{is its supposed domain}}}} \\ (\underbrace{ \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synz^{\syn\iota}} (\synp\synx\syny \synwedge \synp\synx\synz \synimplies \syny \syneq \synz )}_{\synp \T*{induces a single-valued partial function}} \synimplies \underbrace{ \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \synin \synb \syniff \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \synp\synx\syny) }_{\synb \T*{is the image of} \syna \T*{under this function}}).
    \end{multline}
    \normalsize

    We will mainly use the axiom via \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_builder_replacement} or \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/indexed_family}. More often than not, we will use the following first-order adaptation:
    \small
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/replacement}
      \qforall \syna (\underbrace{ \qforall \synx \qforall \syny \qforall \synz (\syn\varphi \synwedge \syn\varphi[\syny \synsubst \synz] \synimplies \syny \syneq \synz) }_{ \syn\varphi \T*{induces a single-valued partial function} } \synimplies \underbrace{ \qexists \synb \qforall \syny (\syny \synin \synb \syniff \qexists \synx \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \syn\varphi) }_{ \synb \T*{is the image of} \syna \T*{under this function} }).
    \end{equation}
    \normalsize

    This is a formula schema in the sense of \cref{def:fol_formula_schema}. We require the \hyperref[con:schemas_and_instances]{instances} of \( \synb \) not to be free in \( \syn\varphi \), but otherwise put no restrictions on the placeholders. Note that we must take the \hyperref[def:fol_quantifier_closure]{universal closure} of each concrete axiom of the schema.

    We use the \enquote{partial function} variant of replacement, also stated in
    \cite[13]{Jech2006SetTheory},
    \cite[179]{Enderton1977SetTheory},
    \cite[243]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof} and
    \cite[\S 1.3.5]{КусраевКутателадзе2005БулевозначныйАнализ}. The \enquote{total function} variant strengthens the antecedent, which in turn allows weakening the consequent:
    \small
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/replacement/total}
      \underbrace{ \qforall {\synx \synin \syna} \qExists \syny \syn\varphi }_{\mathclap{\syn\varphi \T*{induces a total function}}} \synimplies \underbrace{ \qexists \synb \qforall {\synx \synin \syna} \qexists {\syny \synin \synb} \syn\varphi }_{\mathclap{\synb \T*{contains the image of} \syna} }.
    \end{equation}
    \normalsize

    This latter variant is stated in \cite[25]{Kunen2013SetTheory} and \cite[186]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость}. Some discussion on the two variants is given in \cite{MathSE:axiom_schema_of_replacement_partial_vs_total}.

    \thmitem{def:zfc/infinity}\mcite[184]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость} The \term[ru=аксиома бесконечности, en=axiom of infinity (\cite[190]{Kleene2002Logic})]{axiom of infinity} states than an \hyperref[def:inductive_set]{inductive set} exists:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/infinity}
      \qexists \syna \underbrace{ \synpred{inductive}(\syna) }_{ \mathclap{ (\synnothing \synin \syna) \synwedge \qforall {\synx \synin \syna} (\synS \synx \synin \syna) } }.
    \end{equation}

    This axiom uses the \( \synpred{inductive} \) predicate symbol from \cref{def:inductive_set}, which depends on the function symbols \( \synnothing \) from \cref{def:empty_set} and \( \synS \) from \cref{def:ordinal_successor}. Since we will need this axiom to prove the well-definedness of both \( \synnothing \) and \( \synS \), we will initially assume the expanded version
    \small
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/infinity/expanded}
      \qexists \syna (\underbrace{ \qexists {\syne \synin \syna} \qforall \synx \synneg (\synx \synin \syne) }_{\syna \T*{contains the empty set} \syne} \synwedge \underbrace{ \qforall {\synx \synin \syna} \qexists {\syns \synin \syna} \qforall \syny (\syny \synin \syns \syniff \syny \synin \synx \synvee \syny \syneq \synx) }_{\T*{every set} \synx \T*{in} \syna \T*{has a successor} \syns}).
    \end{equation}
    \normalsize

    The axiom of infinity has two important consequences --- the existence of the \hyperref[def:smallest_inductive_set]{smallest inductive set} \( \omega \) (which we show in \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/smallest_inductive_set}), and, in particular, the existence of \emph{any} set. Some authors like \textcite[def. 6.1.6]{Hinman2005Logic} assume the existence of \( \omega \) directly; we can do this by restating the definition of \( \syn\Omega \) from \cref{def:smallest_inductive_set}. We prefer the more popular variant of asserting that at least one inductive set exists; this variant of the axiom is stated in
    \cite[12]{Jech2006SetTheory},
    \cite[17]{Kunen2013SetTheory},
    \cite[146]{Mendelson2015Logic},
    \cite[243]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof},
    \cite[184]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость},
    \cite[159]{КолмогоровДрагалин2006ЛогикаДополнительныеГлавы} and
    \cite[\S 1.3.8]{КусраевКутателадзе2005БулевозначныйАнализ}.

    \thmitem{def:zfc/union}\mcite[183]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость} The \term[ru=аксиома объединения, en=axiom of union (\cite[190]{Kleene2002Logic})]{axiom of union} asserts that existence of \hyperref[def:set_union]{unary unions}:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/union}
      \underbrace{\qforall \syna \qexists \synu \qforall \synx (\synx \synin \synu \syniff \qexists {\syny \synin \synx} \syny \synin \syna)}_{\synu \T*{is the union of} \syna}.
    \end{equation}

    \thmitem{def:zfc/power_set}\mcite[184]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость} The \term[ru=аксиома степени, en=axiom of the power set (\cite[190]{Kleene2002Logic})]{axiom of the power set} asserts that the existence of \hyperref[def:power_set]{power sets}:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/power_set}
      \underbrace{\qforall \syna \qexists \synp \qforall \synx (\synx \synin \synp \syniff \synx \synsubseteq \synp)}_{\synp \T*{is the power set of} \syna}.
    \end{equation}

    We have used the subset predicate symbol \( {\synsubseteq} \) from \cref{def:subset}.

    \thmitem{def:zfc/foundation}\mcite[184]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость} The \term[ru=аксиома фундирования, en=axiom of foundation (\cite[245]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof})]{axiom of foundation} states that set membership is \hyperref[def:well_founded_relation]{well-founded}:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/foundation/expanded}
      \qforall \syna \synpred{well-founded}(\syna).
    \end{equation}
    where
    \begin{equation*}
      \synpred{well-founded}(\syna) \coloneqq \underbrace{ \qexists \synx {\synx \synin \syna} }_{\mathclap{ \syna \T*{is not empty} }} \synimplies \underbrace{ \qexists {\synm \synin \syna} \synneg \qexists {\synx \synin \syna} {\synx \synin \synm} }_{\synm \T*{is minimal in} \syna \T*{with respect to} \synin}.
    \end{equation*}

    The axiom is also sometimes stated in a more cryptic form --- that every nonempty set contains a member disjoint from itself:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/foundation}
      \qforall \syna (\synneg (\syna \syneq \synnothing) \synimplies \qexists {\synm \synin \syna} (\synm \syncap \syna \syneq \synnothing)).
    \end{equation}

    Both forms are listed in \cite[245]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof} and \cite[187]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость}. They are alternatively referred to as the \term[ru=аксиома регулярности, en=axiom of regularity (\cite[\S I.6]{Jech2006SetTheory})]{axiom of regularity} in \cite[\S I.6]{Jech2006SetTheory}, \cite[159]{КолмогоровДрагалин2006ЛогикаДополнительныеГлавы} and \cite[187]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость}, because they are equivalent to \fullref{thm:axiom_of_regularity}.

    \thmitem{def:zfc/choice}\mimprovised The \term[ru=аксиома выбора (\cite[\S 3.4.5]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость}), en=axiom of choice (\cite[190]{Kleene2002Logic})]{axiom of choice} states that a \hyperref[def:choice_function]{choice function} exists for any collection of nonempty sets:
    \begin{multline}\label{eq:def:zfc/choice}
      \qforall \syna \synneg (\synnothing \synin \syna) \synimplies \\ \qexists \synf (\synpred{family}(\synf) \synwedge (\syndom(\synf) \syneq \syna) \synwedge \qforall {\synb \synin \syna} \qexists {\sync \synin \synb} (\synK(\synb, \sync) \synin \synf))).
    \end{multline}

    This concrete formulation is based on \cite[17]{Kunen2013SetTheory}, but modified to reflect \cref{rem:function_definition}, according to which, for simplicity, we use indexed families to state formal axioms where informally we would use functions. We have used the function symbol \( \synK \) from \cref{def:kuratowski_pair} for denoting Kuratowski pairs, as well as the function symbol \( \syndom \) and the predicate symbol \( \synpred{family} \) from \cref{def:indexed_family}.

    By definition, the \hyperref[def:cartesian_product]{Cartesian product} consists of graphs of \hyperref[def:choice_function]{choice functions}, so, given our formalisms, a slightly more accurate interpretation is that every collection of nonempty sets has a nonempty Cartesian product.

    The axiom of choice is generally considered controversial because of far-fetched consequences and unobvious equivalent forms like those we list in \fullref{thm:axiom_of_choice_consequences} and \fullref{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences}, correspondingly. Thomas Jech has an entire book, \cite{Jech1973AxiomOfChoice}, dedicated to this axiom.
  \end{thmenum}

  The following are commonly stated as axioms of \logic{ZFC}, although they can be derived from the above:
  \begin{thmenum}[resume=def:zfc]
    \thmitem{def:zfc/comprehension}\mcite[185]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость} The \term[en=axiom of comprehension (\cite[30]{Schechter1997AnalysisHandbook}), ru=аксиома выделения (\cite[137]{КолмогоровДрагалин2006ЛогикаДополнительныеГлавы})]{axiom of comprehension} asserts the existence of subsets determined by predicates:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/comprehension/sol}
      \overbrace{ \qforall {\synp^{\syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron}} \qforall {\syna^{\syn\iota}} }^{\mathclap{\substack{\synp \T*{is a unary predicate and} \\ \syna \T*{is its supposed domain}}}} \underbrace{ \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synx \synin \synb \syniff \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \synx)}_{\synb \T*{is the subset of} \syna \T*{determined by} \synp}.
    \end{equation}

    We also call it the \term[en=restricted comprehension (\cite[244]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof})]{axiom of restricted comprehension} to highlight its limitation compared to unrestricted comprehension from \hyperref[def:naive_set_theory]{naive axiomatic set theory}. The axiom has several other established names --- see \cref{rem:axiom_of_specification_names}.

    This axiom justifies using \hyperref[def:set_theory_definition/restricted]{restricted set-builder notation} --- see \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_builder}.

    More often than not, we will use the following first-order adaptation:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/comprehension}
      \qforall \syna \underbrace{ \qexists \synb \qforall \synx (\synx \synin \synb \syniff \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \varphi)}_{\synb \T*{is the subset of} \syna \T*{determined by} \varphi}.
    \end{equation}

    Similarly to the axiom schema of replacement, the above is formula schema in the sense of \cref{def:fol_formula_schema}. We require the \hyperref[con:schemas_and_instances]{instances} of \( \synb \) not to be free in \( \syn\varphi \), but otherwise put no restrictions on the placeholders. Note that we must take the \hyperref[def:fol_quantifier_closure]{universal closure} of each concrete axiom of the schema.

    We can derive the (second-order) axiom of specification from the axiom of replacement.

    \thmitem{def:zfc/empty_set}\mcite[180]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость} The \term[ru=аксиома пустого множества]{axiom of the empty set} allows proving that set membership is \hyperref[def:well_founded_relation]{well-founded}:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/empty_set}
      \qexists \syne (\qforall \synx \synneg (\synx \synin \syne)).
    \end{equation}

    This axiom is rarely stated, but can be found in
    \cite[180]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость},
    \cite[159]{КолмогоровДрагалин2006ЛогикаДополнительныеГлавы}
    and, for \hyperref[rem:set_theory_acronyms/nbg]{\logic{NBG}}, in
    \cite[xix]{Mendelson2015Logic}.

    We can derive the axiom of the empty set from the axiom of infinity.

    \thmitem{def:zfc/pairing}\mcite[181]{Герасимов2014Вычислимость} The \term[ru=аксиома пары, en=axiom of pairing (\cite[190]{Kleene2002Logic})]{axiom of pairing} asserts the existence of \hyperref[def:unordered_pair]{unordered pairs}:
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/pairing}
      \qforall \syna \qforall \synb \qexists \synp \qforall \synx (\synx \synin \synp \syniff (\synx \synin \syna \synvee \synx \synin \synb)).
    \end{equation}

    As per \cref{thm:roster_notation_via_pairs}, along with the axiom of union, this axioms justifies \hyperref[def:set_theory_definition/roster]{roster notation}.

    We can derive the axiom of pairing from the axiom of the empty set, the axiom of the power set and an instance of the axiom of replacement.
  \end{thmenum}
\end{definition}

\begin{defproof}
  \SubProofOf{def:zfc/comprehension} The instance of the second-order axiom of specification with \( \synp^{\syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron} \) follows from the axiom of replacement, which we instantiate with
  \begin{equation*}
    M \coloneqq \qabs {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \qabs {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\synx \syneq \syny \synwedge \synp \synx).
  \end{equation*}
  to obtain
  \small
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:def:zfc/proof/comprehension/replacement_instance}
    \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synz^{\syn\iota}} (M \synx \syny \synwedge M \synx \synz \synimplies \syny \syneq \synz) \synimplies \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \synin \synb \syniff \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synx \synin \syna \synwedge M \synx\syny).
  \end{equation}
  \normalsize

  The antecedent is simple to derive:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{ [M \synx \syny \synwedge M \synx \synz]^u }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_right}]{ M \synx \synz }

      \infer0[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/intro}]{ M \synx \synz \syneq (\synx \syneq \synz \synwedge \synp \synx) }

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/elim}]{ \synx \syneq \synz \synwedge \synp \synx }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_left}]{ \synx \syneq \synz }

      \hypo{ [M \synx \syny \synwedge M \synx \synz]^u }
      \ellipsis {} {\synx \syneq \syny}

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/elim}]{ \syny \syneq \synz }
      \infer[left label=\( u \)]1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/imp/intro}]{ M \synx\syny \synwedge M \synx\synz \synimplies \syny \syneq \synz }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:fol_natural_deduction/forall/intro}*]{ \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synz^{\syn\iota}} (M \synx\syny \synwedge M \synx\synz \synimplies \syny \syneq \synz) }
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}

  This leaves us with the consequent of \eqref{eq:def:zfc/proof/comprehension/replacement_instance}. We can use it to derive \eqref{eq:def:zfc/comprehension/sol} via the tree
  \small
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{}
      \ellipsis {} { \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \synin \synb \syniff \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synx \synin \syna \synwedge M \synx\syny) }

      \hypo{ \substack{ [\qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \synin \synb \syniff \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synx \synin \syna \synwedge M \synx\syny)]^u \\ [\syny \synin \synb]^v } }
      \ellipsis{\( L \)} { \syny \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \syny }

      \hypo{ \substack{ [\qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \synin \synb \syniff \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synx \synin \syna \synwedge M \synx\syny)]^u \\ [\syny \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \syny]^w } }
      \ellipsis{\( R \)} { \syny \synin \synb }

      \infer[left label={\( v, w \)}]2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/iff/intro}]{ \syny \synin \synb \syniff \syny \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \syny }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/intro}]{ \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synx \synin \synb \syniff \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \synx) }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/exists/intro}]{ \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synx \synin \synb \syniff \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \synx) }
      \infer[left label={\( u \)}]2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/exists/elim}]{ \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synx \synin \synb \syniff \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \synx) }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/intro}]{ \qforall {\syna^{\syn\iota}} \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synx \synin \synb \syniff \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \synx) }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/intro}]{ \qforall {\synp^{\syn\iota \synimplies \syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron}} \qforall {\syna^{\syn\iota}} \qexists {\synb^{\syn\iota}} \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synx \synin \synb \syniff \synx \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \synx) }
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \normalsize
  where \( L \) is
  \footnotesize
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}[separation=0.9em]
      \hypo{ [\synanon]^u }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/elim}]{ \synanon }

      \hypo{ [\syny \synin \synb]^v }

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/iff/elim_right}]{ \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synx \synin \syna \synwedge M \synx\syny }

      \hypo{ [\synx \synin \syna \synwedge M \synx\syny]^t }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_left}]{ \synx \synin \syna }

      \hypo{ [\synanon]^t }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_right}]{ M \synx \syny }

      \infer0[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/intro}]{ M \synx \syny \syneq (\synx \syneq \syny \synwedge \synp \synx) }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/elim}]{ \synx \syneq \syny \synwedge \synp \synx }

      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_left}]{ \synx \syneq \syny }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/elim}]{ \syny \synin \syna }

      \hypo{ [\synanon]^t }
      \ellipsis {} { \synp \synx }

      \hypo{ [\synanon]^t }
      \ellipsis {} { \synx \syneq \syny }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/elim}]{ \synp \syny }

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/intro}]{ \syny \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \syny }

      \infer[left label={\( t \)}]2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/exists/elim}]{ \syny \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \syny }
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \normalsize
  and \( R \) is
  \small
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{ [\synanon]^u }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/elim}]{ \synanon }

      \hypo{ [\syny \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \syny]^w }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_left}]{ \syny \synin \syna }

      \infer0[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/intro}]{ \syny \syneq \syny }

      \hypo{ [\syny \synin \syna \synwedge \synp \syny]^w }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_right}]{ \synp \syny }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/intro}]{ \syny \syneq \syny \synwedge \synp \syny }

      \infer0[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/intro}]{ (\syny \syneq \syny \synwedge \synp \syny) \syneq M \syny \syny }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/equality/elim}]{ M \syny \syny }

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/intro}]{ \syny \synin \syna \synwedge M \syny \syny }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/exists/intro}]{ \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synx \synin \syna \synwedge M \synx \syny }

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/iff/elim_left}]{ \syny \synin \syna }
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \normalsize

  \SubProofOf{def:zfc/empty_set} The expanded variant \eqref{eq:def:zfc/infinity/expanded} of the axiom of infinity contains the axiom of the empty set almost verbatim:
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}
      \hypo{ \eqref{eq:def:zfc/infinity/expanded} }

      \hypo{ \synanon }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_left}]{ \qexists {\syne \synin \syna} \qforall \synx \synneg (\synx \synin \syne) }

      \hypo{ \syne \synin \syna \synwedge \qforall \synx \synneg (\synx \synin \syne) }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_right}]{ \qforall \synx \synneg (\synx \synin \syne) }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:fol_natural_deduction/exists/intro}]{ \eqref{eq:def:zfc/empty_set} }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:fol_natural_deduction/exists/elim}]{ \synanon }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:fol_natural_deduction/exists/elim}]{ \synanon }
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}

  \SubProofOf{def:zfc/pairing} The axioms of the empty set and of the power set allow working with \( \synpow(\synpow(\synnothing)) \), whose members are \( \synnothing \) and \( \synpow(\synnothing) \).

  The axiom of pairing follows from the axiom schema of replacement, where \( \syna \) is instantiated with \( \synpow(\synpow(\synnothing)) \) and \( \syn\varphi \) instantiated with
  \begin{equation*}
    (\synx \syneq \synnothing \synimplies \syny \syneq \syna) \synwedge (\synx \syneq \synpow(\synnothing) \synimplies \syny \syneq \synb).
  \end{equation*}

  The consequent of the axiom of replacement becomes
  \begin{equation*}
    \qexists \synp \qforall \syny (\syny \synin \synp \syniff \underbrace{\qexists {\overbrace{\synx \synin \syna}^{\mathclap{\synx \syneq \synnothing \synvee \synx \syneq \synpow(\synnothing)}}} ((\synx \syneq \synnothing \synimplies \syny \syneq \syna) \synwedge (\synx \syneq \synpow(\synnothing) \synimplies \syny \syneq \synb))}_{\syny \syneq \syna \synvee \syny \syneq \synb}),
  \end{equation*}
  which is the the abbreviation we have introduced in \cref{def:set_theory_definition/roster} for the unordered pair \( \set{ \syna, \synb } \).

  The antecedent of the axiom of replacement, which requires proving uniqueness, is straightforward to show.
\end{defproof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:first_order_zfc}
  In \cref{def:axiomatic_set_theory}, we mentioned that set theory is often presented as the \hyperref[def:fol_adaptation_of_monadic_sol_theory]{first-order adaptation} of a \hyperref[def:second_order_logic]{monadic second-order} \hyperref[def:hol_theory]{theory}.

  In fact, in the literature we cite, \hyperref[def:zfc]{Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory} is presented only as a (unisorted) first-order theory; namely, in
  \cite[ch. I]{Kunen2013SetTheory},
  \cite[ch. I.1]{Jech2006SetTheory},
  \cite[\S 6.1]{Hinman2005Logic},
  \cite[\S 5.3]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof},
  \cite[\S I.2]{КолмогоровДрагалин2006ЛогикаДополнительныеГлавы},

  \cite[\S 1.3]{КусраевКутателадзе2005БулевозначныйАнализ}.

  \Textcite{Enderton1977SetTheory} and \textcite{Halmos1960NaiveSetTheory} are not explicit about the underlying logical system, but their presentation only fits first-order logic.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}\label{thm:zfc_existence_theorems}
  We will now prove the well-definedness of all \hyperref[def:fol_definition/function]{defined function symbols} we have considered thus far in \fullref{ch:set_theory}, as well as of some convenience \hyperref[con:syntactic_abbreviation]{abbreviations}.

  These constructs are ill-defined unless we can justify them, so a fully formal treatment would require painstakingly restating and expanding their definitions. Instead, for convenience, we will use an informal metalanguage tantamount to to working in an unspecified model of \logic{ZFC}.

  \begin{thmenum}
    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/empty_set} The \hyperref[def:empty_set]{empty set} \( \varnothing \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/singleton} For any set \( A \), there is a well-defined a \hyperref[def:singleton_set]{singleton set} \( \set{ A } \) that contains only \( A \).

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/unordered_pair} For any two sets sets \( A \) and \( B \), their \hyperref[def:unordered_pair]{unordered pair} \( \set{ A, B } \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/unary_union} For any collection of sets \( \mscrA \), the \hyperref[def:set_union/unary]{unary union} \( \bigcup \mscrA \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/binary_union} For any two sets \( A \) and \( B \), their \hyperref[def:set_union/binary]{binary union} \( A \cup B \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/roster} Roster notation, as introduced in \cref{def:set_theory_definition/roster}, is well-defined --- for every (metatheoretic) tuple \( A_1, \ldots, A_n \) of sets, there is a well-defined a unique set containing them:
    \begin{equation*}
      \set{ A_1, \ldots, A_n }
    \end{equation*}

    In particular, when \( n = 0 \), we obtain the empty set.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_builder_replacement} Set-builder notation with functions, as introduced in \cref{def:set_theory_definition/function}, is well-defined --- any metatheoretic endofunction \( F \) on sets given by a first-order term can be used to define an \enquote{image} of an arbitrary set \( A \):
    \begin{equation*}
      \set{ F(x) \given x \in A }.
    \end{equation*}

    When dealing with the second-order theory \( \logic{ZFC}_2 \), \( F \) may be a more general endofunction than what is allowed by a first-order term.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_builder} Restricted set-builder notation, as introduced in \cref{def:set_theory_definition/restricted}, is well-defined --- any metatheoretic predicate \( P \) given by a first-order formula induces a subset for an arbitrary set \( A \):
    \begin{equation*}
      \set{ x \in A \given P(x) }.
    \end{equation*}

    When dealing with the second-order theory \( \logic{ZFC}_2 \), \( P \) may be more general than what is allowed by a first-order formula.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/unary_intersection} For any \emph{nonempty} collection of sets \( \mscrA \), the \hyperref[def:set_intersection/unary]{intersection} \( \bigcap \mscrA \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/binary_intersection} For any two sets \( A \) and \( B \), their \hyperref[def:set_intersection]{intersection} \( A \cap B \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/difference} For any two sets \( A \) and \( B \), their \hyperref[def:set_difference]{difference} \( A \setminus B \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/power_set} For any set \( A \), the \hyperref[def:power_set]{power set} \( \pow(A) \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/successor} For any set \( A \), the \hyperref[def:ordinal_successor]{ordinal successor} \( S A \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/smallest_inductive_set} The \hyperref[def:smallest_inductive_set]{smallest inductive set} \( \omega \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/kuratowski_pair} For any two sets \( A \) and \( B \), their \hyperref[def:kuratowski_pair]{Kuratowski pair} \( \braket{ A, B } = \set{ \set{ A }, \set{ A, B } } \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/indexed_family} Fix a set \( \mscrK \) and a binary metatheoretic predicate \( P \) that can be expressed via a first-order formula. Suppose that, for every member \( k \) of \( \mscrK \), there exists a unique set \( A_k \) such that \( P(k, A_k) = \semtop \). Then the \hyperref[def:indexed_family]{indexed family} \( \seq{ A_k }_{k \in \mscrK} \) is well-defined.

    When dealing with the second-order theory \( \logic{ZFC}_2 \), \( P \) may be more general than what is allowed by a first-order formula.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/cartesian_product} For any indexed family \( \set{ A_k }_{k \in \mscrK} \), its \hyperref[def:cartesian_product]{Cartesian product} \( \prod_{k \in \mscrK} A_k \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/disjoint_union} For any indexed family \( \set{ A_k }_{k \in \mscrK} \), its \hyperref[def:disjoint_union]{disjoint union} \( \coprod_{k \in \mscrK} A_k \) is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_of_functions} For any two sets \( A \) and \( B \), the set \hyperref[def:set_of_all_functions]{\( \fun(A, B) \)} of functions between them is well-defined.

    \thmitem{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/quotient_set} For any set \( A \) and any \hyperref[def:equivalence_relation]{equivalence relation} \( {\sim} \) on \( A \), the \hyperref[def:equivalence_relation/quotient]{quotient set} \( A / {\sim} \) is well-defined.
  \end{thmenum}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/empty_set} Restatement of the axiom of the empty set.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/singleton} Follows from the axiom of pairing.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/unordered_pair} Restatement of the axiom of pairing.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/unary_union} Restatement of the axiom of union.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/binary_union} \Cref{thm:binary_set_union_via_pair} implies that
  \begin{equation*}
    A \cup B = \bigcup \set{ A, B }.
  \end{equation*}

  Thus, the existence of the binary union \( A \cup B \) is justified by the axiom of union and the axiom of pairing.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/roster} By (metatheoretic) induction on \( n \), we can show the well-definedness of
  \begin{equation*}
    \set{ A_1, \ldots, A_n }.
  \end{equation*}

  \begin{itemize}
    \item For \( n = 0 \), existence of the empty set follows by \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/empty_set}.
    \item If \( \set{ A_1, \ldots, A_{n - 1} } \) is well-defined, then \cref{thm:roster_notation_via_pairs} implies that
    \begin{equation*}
      \set{ A_1, \ldots, A_{n-1}, A_n } = \set{ A_1, \ldots, A_{n-1} } \syncup \set{ A_n }.
    \end{equation*}

    The singleton set \( \set{ A_n } \) is well-defined by \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/singleton}, and the union is well-defined by \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/binary_union}.
  \end{itemize}

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_builder_replacement} Restatement of the axiom (schema) of replacement.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_builder} Restatement of the axiom (schema) of comprehension.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/unary_intersection} Let \( \mscrA \) be a nonempty family of sets. Then \( \mscrA \) contains some set \( A_0 \), and
  \begin{equation*}
    \bigcap \mscrA = \set{ x \in A_0 \given \qexists* {A \in \mscrA} x \in A }.
  \end{equation*}

  Thus, the existence of \( \bigcap \mscrA \) follows from the axiom schema of comprehension (or \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_builder}).

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/binary_intersection} Again, we use comprehension:
  \begin{equation*}
    A \cap B = \set{ x \given x \in A \T{and} x \in B } = \set{ x \in A \given x \in B }.
  \end{equation*}

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/difference} Again, we use comprehension:
  \begin{equation*}
    A \setminus B = \set{ x \given x \in A \T{and} x \notin B } = \set{ x \in A \given x \notin B }.
  \end{equation*}

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/power_set} Restatement of the axiom of power sets.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/successor} The successor of \( A \) is defined as
  \begin{equation*}
    SA = \set{ A } \cup A.
  \end{equation*}

  \Cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/singleton} implies that \( \set{ A } \) is well-defined and \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/binary_union} implies that its union with \( A \) is also well-defined.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/smallest_inductive_set} By the axiom of infinity, there exists at least one inductive set \( I \). The collection
  \begin{equation*}
    \mscrI \coloneqq \set{ A \subseteq I \given A \T{is inductive} } = \set{ A \in \pow(I) \given A \T{is inductive} }
  \end{equation*}
  is well-defined by the axiom of the power set and the axiom (schema) of comprehension.

  This collection is nonempty since it contains \( I \) itself, so, by \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/unary_intersection}, its intersection
  \begin{equation*}
    \omega \coloneqq \bigcap \mscrI = \bigcap \set{ A \subseteq I \given A \T{is inductive} }
  \end{equation*}
  is well-defined. Furthermore, \( \omega \) is inductive as the intersection of inductive sets.

  It remains to show that \( \omega \) satisfies \cref{def:smallest_inductive_set}, i.e. that its members are precisely those sets that are contained in every inductive set (informally, \( \omega \) must be the intersection of all inductive sets).
  \begin{itemize}
    \item If \( n \) belongs to all inductive sets, then it belongs to \( \omega \) in particular.

    \item Conversely, if \( n \) belongs to \( \omega \), it belongs to any inductive set \( J \). Indeed, the intersection \( I \cap J \) is inductive, so it belongs to \( \mscrI \). \Cref{thm:unary_set_intersection_inequality} implies that \( \omega \) is a subset of \( I \cap J \), and hence of \( J \). Thus, \( n \) belongs to \( J \).
  \end{itemize}

  This concludes the proof.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/kuratowski_pair} By the axiom of pairing, \( \set{ A } \) and \( \set{ A, B } \) are well-defined, and hence so is their pair
  \begin{equation*}
    \braket{ A, B } = \set{ \set{ A }, \set{ A, B } }.
  \end{equation*}

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/indexed_family} We use the axiom (schema) of replacement, but instead of using \( P \) directly, we use\fnote{We have defined \( Q(k, B) \) in the metatheory; both the first-order logic and higher-order logic syntactic counterparts of \( Q \) are straightforward.}
  \begin{equation*}
     Q(k, B) = \begin{cases}
       \semtop &\T{there exists a set} A_k \T{such that} B = \braket{ k, A_k } \T{and} P(k, A_k) = \semtop, \\
       \sembot &\T{otherwise.}
     \end{cases}
  \end{equation*}

  Replacement gives us a set \( \mscrA \) of Kuratowski pairs \( \braket{ k, A_k } \) such that \( P(k, A_k) = \semtop \). This set \( \mscrA \) is the desired indexed family.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/cartesian_product} We discuss the encoding of Cartesian products in \logic{ZFC} in \cref{rem:cartesian_product_existence}. In short, we must use comprehension to single out \( \set{ A_k }_{k \in \mscrK} \) as a subset of
  \begin{equation*}
    \pow^3\parens*{ \mscrK \cup \bigcup_{k \in \mscrK} A_k }.
  \end{equation*}

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/disjoint_union} We have defined disjoint unions in \cref{def:disjoint_union} as sets of Kuratowski pairs.

  As shown in \cref{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/kuratowski_pair}, the \hyperref[def:zfc/pairing]{axiom of pairing} allows us to construct a pair \( \braket{ k, a } \) for every \( k \) in \( \mscrK \) and every \( a \) in \( A_k \).

  Then, by the axiom (schema) of replacement, we can form the disjoint union as the set of all such pairs.

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/set_of_functions} We discuss the encoding of \( \fun(A, B) \) products in \logic{ZFC} in \cref{rem:set_of_all_functions_existence}. In short, we must use comprehension to single out \( \fun(A, B) \) as a subset of
  \begin{equation*}
    \pow(A \times B) \times \set{ A } \times \set{ B }.
  \end{equation*}

  \SubProofOf{thm:zfc_existence_theorems/quotient_set} In \cref{def:equivalence_relation/quotient}, we have defined the quotient \( A / {\cong} \) as
  \begin{equation*}
    A / {\cong} = \set{ [x] \given x \in A }.
  \end{equation*}

  For every \( x \) in \( A \), there exists a unique subset \( [x] \) of \( A \) consisting of all \( y \) in \( A \) for which \( x \sim y \). This allows us to use the axiom (schema) of replacement to define \( A / {\cong} \).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Axiom of choice}

\begin{theorem}[Existence of single-valued selections]\label{thm:existence_of_single_valued_selections}
  If a \hyperref[def:set_valued_function]{set-valued function} is not \hyperref[def:set_valued_function/value_cardinality/empty]{empty-valued} anywhere, it has a \hyperref[def:set_valued_selection]{single-valued selection}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{comments}
  \item Within \hyperref[def:zfc]{\logic{ZF}}, this theorem is equivalent to the \hyperref[def:zfc/choice]{axiom of choice}. We list equivalent results in \cref{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences}.
\end{comments}
\begin{proof}
  \ImplicationSubProof[def:zfc/choice]{the axiom of choice}[thm:existence_of_single_valued_selections]{selection existence} Let \( F: A \multto B \) be a set-valued map that is not empty-valued anywhere. Then \( F[A] = \set{ F(a) \given a \in A } \) is a (possibly empty) collection of nonempty sets. Thus, we can apply the axiom of choice to obtain some \hyperref[def:choice_function]{choice function}
  \begin{equation*}
    c: \set{ F(a) \given a \in A } \to B.
  \end{equation*}

  Then the following is a selection of \( F \):
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
      &f: A \to B, \\
      &f(a) \coloneqq c(F(a)).
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation*}

  \ImplicationSubProof[thm:existence_of_single_valued_selections]{selection existence}[def:zfc/choice]{axiom of choice} Suppose that every set-valued function that is not empty-valued anywhere has a single-valued selection.

  Fix a family \( \mscrA \) of nonempty sets and define the function
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
      &F: \mscrA \multto \bigcup \mscrA, \\
      &F(A) \coloneqq A
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation*}
  that sends each set in \( \mscrA \) to the corresponding subset of \( \bigcup \mscrA \). In terms of relations, we have \( (A, x) \in \gr(F) \) if and only if \( x \in A \).

  Then every selection of \( F \) is a choice function for \( \mscrA \).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Surjective functions are right invertible]\label{thm:surjective_functions_are_right_invertible}
  Every \hyperref[def:function_invertibility/surjective]{surjective function} is \hyperref[def:morphism_invertibility/right]{right invertible}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{comments}
  \item Within \hyperref[def:zfc]{\logic{ZF}}, this theorem is equivalent to the \hyperref[def:zfc/choice]{axiom of choice}. We list equivalent results in \cref{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences}.
\end{comments}
\begin{proof}
  Let \( f: A \to B \) be any function. Its \hyperref[def:set_valued_function/inverse]{inverse} \( f^{-1}: B \multto A \) is, by definition, a set-valued function.

  \ImplicationSubProof[def:zfc/choice]{the axiom of choice}[thm:epimorphisms_split_in_set]{right-invertibility} Suppose that \( f \) is surjective and thus its inverse is not empty-valued anywhere.

  Then the axiom of choice via \fullref{thm:existence_of_single_valued_selections} gives us a \hyperref[def:set_valued_selection]{single-valued selection} \( g \) of \( f^{-1} \).

  Since the value of \( f \) is \( y \) for all members of \( f^{-1}(y) \), and since \( g(y) \in f^{-1}(y) \), we have
  \begin{equation*}
    [f \bincirc g](y) = f(g(y)) = y.
  \end{equation*}

  Therefore, \( g \) is a right inverse of \( f \).

  \ImplicationSubProof[thm:epimorphisms_split_in_set]{right-invertibility}[def:zfc/choice]{the axiom of choice} Suppose that every surjective function is invertible.

  Let \( \mscrA \) be an arbitrary family of nonempty sets. Define the function
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
      &f: \coprod_{A \in \mscrA} A \to \mscrA, \\
      &f(A, x) \coloneqq A,
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation*}
  where \( \coprod \) denotes the \hyperref[def:disjoint_union]{disjoint union}.

  This function is surjective, thus there exists some right-inverse
  \begin{equation*}
    g: \mscrA \to \coprod_{A \in \mscrA} A.
  \end{equation*}

  For a fixed set \( A \) from the family \( \mscrA \), \( g(A) \) is a pair \( (A, x) \) with \( x \in A \). This allows us to define the choice function
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{aligned}
      &c: \mscrA \to \bigcup \mscrA, \\
      &c(A) \coloneqq x \T{where} g(A) = (A, x).
    \end{aligned}
  \end{equation*}

  Since the family \( \mscrA \) was arbitrary, we can conclude that the axiom of choice holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Axiom of choice consequences]\label{thm:axiom_of_choice_consequences}
  The following statements are considered consequences of the \hyperref[def:zfc/choice]{axiom of choice}. As a rule of thumb, they hold in \hyperref[def:zfc]{\logic{ZFC}} but not \logic{ZF}.

  \begin{thmenum}
    \thmitem{thm:thm:axiom_of_choice_consequences/diaconescus_theorem} \Fullref{thm:diaconescu_goodman_myhill_theorem}: In \hyperref[def:zfc]{\logic{ZF}}, the \hyperref[def:zfc/choice]{axiom of choice} entails the law of the excluded middle \eqref{eq:thm:classical_tautologies/lem}.

    \thmitem{thm:thm:axiom_of_choice_consequences/ultrafilter_lemma} \Fullref{thm:ultrafilter_lemma}: Every proper \hyperref[def:lattice_ideal]{filter} in a \hyperref[def:boolean_algebra]{Boolean algebra} is contained in an \hyperref[def:ultrafilter]{ultrafilter}.
  \end{thmenum}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Axiom of choice equivalences]\label{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences}
  The following statements are equivalent to \hyperref[def:zfc/choice]{axiom of choice} in \hyperref[def:zfc]{\logic{ZF}}:
  \begin{thmenum}[resume=thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences]
    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/zorns_lemma} \Fullref{thm:zorns_lemma}: If every \hyperref[def:partial_order_connectedness]{chain} in a \hyperref[def:partially_ordered_set]{partially ordered set} has an \hyperref[def:extremal_points/bounds]{upper bound}, then the entire set has a \hyperref[def:extremal_points/maximal_and_minimal_element]{maximal element}.

    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/well_ordering} \Fullref{thm:well_ordering_theorem}: Every \hyperref[def:set]{set} can be \hyperref[def:well_ordered_set]{well-ordered}.

    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/maximal_ideal} \Fullref{thm:maximal_ideal_theorem}: Every proper \hyperref[def:semiring_ideal]{semiring ideal} is contained in a \hyperref[def:semiring_ideal/maximal]{maximal ideal}.

    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/tychonoff} \Fullref{thm:tychonoffs_product_theorem}: The \hyperref[def:topological_product]{topological product} of \hyperref[def:compact_space]{compact spaces} is compact.

    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/selection} \Fullref{thm:existence_of_single_valued_selections}: If a \hyperref[def:set_valued_function]{set-valued function} is not \hyperref[def:set_valued_function/value_cardinality/empty]{empty-valued} anywhere, it has a \hyperref[def:set_valued_selection]{single-valued selection}.

    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/surjective} \Fullref{thm:surjective_functions_are_right_invertible}: Every \hyperref[def:function_invertibility/surjective]{surjective function} is \hyperref[def:morphism_invertibility/right]{right invertible}.

    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/epimorphisms} \Fullref{thm:epimorphisms_split_in_set}: Every \hyperref[def:morphism_cancellation/right]{epimorphism} in \hyperref[def:category_of_small_sets]{\( \cat{Set} \)} splits.

    \thmitem{thm:axiom_of_choice_equivalences/skeletons} \Fullref{thm:category_skeleton_existence}: Every \hyperref[def:category]{category} has a \hyperref[def:skeletal_category]{skeleton}.
  \end{thmenum}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}

\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Diaconescu-Goodman-Myhill theorem]\label{thm:diaconescu_goodman_myhill_theorem}\mcite{GoodmanMyhill1978AxiomOfChoice}
  In \hyperref[def:zfc]{\logic{ZF}}, the \hyperref[def:zfc/choice]{axiom of choice} entails the law of the excluded middle \eqref{eq:thm:classical_tautologies/lem}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{comments}
  \item \Textcite[corr. 2]{Diaconescu1975AxiomOfChoice} formulated a more abstract result, which \textcite{GoodmanMyhill1978AxiomOfChoice} later vastly simplified for the case of intuitionistic set theory. Our proof is based on the one given by \textcite[thm. 5.3.3.6]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof}, which in turn is based on the one by Goodman and Myhill.

  \item This is considered a notable consequences of the axiom of choice. We list such consequences in \cref{thm:axiom_of_choice_consequences}.
\end{comments}
\begin{proof}
  A full natural deduction proof tree would be overwhelming, so we only sketch how to build a proof tree of \( \varphi \synvee \synneg \varphi \) for an arbitrary formula \( \varphi \).

  By the axiom of the empty set, the constant \( \synnothing \) is well-defined. By the axiom of the power set, the function symbol \( \pow \) is also well-defined. As per \cref{rem:set_theory_natural_numbers_without_operations}, denote \( \synnothing \) by \( 0 \), \( \pow(\synnothing) \) by \( 1 \). Using the axiom of pairing, we can form the set \( 2 \coloneqq \set{ 0, 1 } \).

  By the axiom schema of comprehension, we can also form the sets
  \begin{align*}
    A &\coloneqq \set[\big]{ \synx \synin 2 \given* (\synx \syneq 0) \synvee \varphi }, \\
    B &\coloneqq \set[\big]{ \synx \synin 2 \given* (\synx \syneq 1) \synvee \varphi }.
  \end{align*}

  Both are nonempty since we can easily derive \( 0 \synin A \) and \( 1 \synin B \). Using the axiom of pairing, we can form \( P \coloneqq \set{ A, B } \). Clearly we can derive \( \synbigcup P \syneq 2 \).

  By the axiom of choice, there exists a choice function \( C \) on \( P \) with values in \( 2 \), which is formally an indexed family, i.e. a set of Kuratowski pairs. We have several possibilities:

  \begin{itemize}
    \item From \( \braket{ A, 1 } \synin C \) we can derive \( 1 \synin A \). There are two cases in the disjunction in \( A \): either we derive \( \varphi \) directly or from \( 1 \syneq 0 \) via \ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/bot/efq}.

    \item The case \( \braket{ B, 0 } \synin C \) is similar --- it leads to \( \varphi \).

    \item Only the case with \( \braket{ A, 0 } \synin C \) and \( \braket{ B, 1 } \synin C \) remains. Because of the uniqueness of the entries of \( C \), we can derive a contradiction from \( A \syneq B \). So either \( 1 \synin A \) or \( 0 \synin B \) leads to a contradiction. Both are consequences of \( \varphi \), so \( \varphi \) itself leads to a contradiction.

    Therefore, via \ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/neg/intro}, in this case we can derive \( \synneg \varphi \).
  \end{itemize}
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Well-founded induction}

\begin{definition}\label{def:well_founded_relation}
  The equivalent descending chain conditions from \cref{def:chain_condition} make sense more generally for an arbitrary \hyperref[def:binary_relation]{binary relation} \( {\prec} \) on \( X \), regarded as a \hyperref[def:partially_ordered_set/strict]{strict order}. We call \( {\prec} \) \term{well-founded} if any of the following equivalent conditions hold:

  \begin{thmenum}
    \thmitem{def:well_founded_relation/minimal}\mcite[25]{Jech2006SetTheory} \Cref{def:chain_condition/maximal}: every nonempty subset \( A \) of \( X \) has a \enquote{minimal} element \( m \) such that \( x \prec m \) does not hold for any \( x \) in \( A \).

    \thmitem{def:well_founded_relation/infinite}\mcite[198]{Shoenfield1967MathematicalLogic} \Cref{def:chain_condition/infinite}: \( A \) contains no strictly descending sequence.
  \end{thmenum}
\end{definition}
\begin{comments}
  \item Sets equipped with well-founded relations generalize \hyperref[def:well_ordered_set]{well-ordered sets}.
\end{comments}
\begin{defproof}
  \EquivalenceSubProof{def:well_founded_relation/minimal}{def:well_founded_relation/infinite} Simply note that \( X \) has a strictly descending sequence
  \begin{equation*}
    \cdots \prec x_n \prec \cdots \prec x_2 \prec x_1
  \end{equation*}
  if and only if all its members are contained in a subset \( A \) of \( X \) with no minimal element.
\end{defproof}

\begin{proposition}\label{thm:set_membership_is_well_founded}
  With the \hyperref[def:zfc/foundation]{axiom of foundation}, set membership becomes \hyperref[def:well_founded_relation]{well-founded} as a relation on a given \hyperref[def:set_theory_model]{model of set theory}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
  The axiom of foundation in the form \eqref{eq:def:zfc/foundation/expanded} corresponds exactly to condition \cref{def:well_founded_relation/minimal}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{thm:simple_foundation_theorems}
  We list several consequences of the \hyperref[def:zfc/foundation]{axiom of foundation}:

  \begin{thmenum}
    \thmitem{thm:simple_foundation_theorems/member_of_itself} No set is a member of itself.

    \thmitem{thm:simple_foundation_theorems/mutual_membership} For any two sets \( A \) and \( B \), it is not possible for both \( A \in B \) and \( B \in A \) to hold.

    \thmitem{thm:simple_foundation_theorems/ordinal_successor_is_injective} The \hyperref[def:ordinal_successor]{ordinal successor operation} is injective: for every pair of sets \( A \) and \( B \), from \( SA = SB \) it follows that \( A = B \).

    This statement generalizes \eqref{eq:def:peano_arithmetic/theory/PA1}, which we have already shown in \cref{thm:omega_is_model_of_pa_without_operations} for members of \( \omega \).
  \end{thmenum}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
  \SubProofOf{thm:simple_foundation_theorems/member_of_itself} If \( A \in A \), then the sequence
  \begin{equation*}
    \cdots A \in A \in A
  \end{equation*}
  is infinitely descending, contradicting well-foundedness of set membership.

  \SubProofOf{thm:simple_foundation_theorems/mutual_membership} If \( A \in B \) and \( B \in A \), then the sequence
  \begin{equation*}
    \cdots A \in B \in A \in B \in A
  \end{equation*}
  is infinitely descending, contradicting well-foundedness of set membership.

  \SubProofOf{thm:simple_foundation_theorems/ordinal_successor_is_injective} Suppose that \( SA = SB \). Since \( A \in SA \), we conclude that \( A \in SB = B \cup \set{ B } \), so either \( A \in B \) or \( A = B \).

  Similarly, we conclude that either \( B \in A \) or \( B = A \). But \( A \in B \) and \( B \in A \) cannot simultaneously hold due to \cref{thm:simple_foundation_theorems/mutual_membership}. Hence, it remains for \( A = B \) to hold.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Well-founded induction]\label{thm:well_founded_induction}
  To properly formula this principle, we will use \hyperref[def:second_order_logic]{monadic second-order logic}, although we will mostly be interested in its \hyperref[def:fol_adaptation_of_monadic_sol_theory]{first-order adaptation}.

  We will work in a fixed \hyperref[def:hol_theory]{theory} \( T \). Let \( {\prec} \) be a binary predicate, i.e. a \hyperref[def:hol_signature/nl_const]{nonlogical constant} of type \( \syn\iota \synimplies \syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron \). This will usually be set membership, however it is also useful for other relations.

  Suppose that \( {\prec} \) is \hyperref[def:well_founded_relation]{well-founded} in the sense that it satisfies an analog of the \hyperref[def:zfc/foundation]{axiom of foundation}:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:thm:well_founded_induction/condition}
    \qforall {\synp^{\syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron}} (\underbrace{ \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} {\synp \synx} }_{\mathclap{ \synp \T*{is not contradictory} }} \synimplies \underbrace{ \qexists {\synm^{\syn\iota}} (\synp \synm \synwedge \synneg \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synp \synx \synwedge \synx \prec \synm) }_{\synm \T*{is minimal among those satisfying} \synp}).
  \end{equation}

  Then we can derive the following induction principle:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:thm:well_founded_induction}
    \qforall {\synp^{\syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron}}
    \parens[\big]
      {
        \parens[\big]
        {
          \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}}
          \overbrace
            {
              (\underbrace{ \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \prec \synx \synimplies \synp \syny) }_{\mathclap{\substack{\T{inductive} \\ \T{hypothesis}}}})
              \synimplies
              \underbrace{ \synp \synx }_{\mathclap{\substack{\T{inductive step} \\ \T{conclusion}}}}
            }^{\T{inductive step}}
        }
        \synimplies
        \underbrace{ \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synp \synx }_{\T{conclusion}}
      }.
  \end{equation}

  Informally, it can be restated as follows:
  \begin{displayquote}
    In order for \( p(x) \) to hold \emph{for every} \( x \), it is sufficient for \( p(x) \) to hold \emph{whenever} \( p(y) \) holds for every \( y \prec x \).
  \end{displayquote}
\end{theorem}
\begin{comments}
  \item With less formality, well-founded induction is discussed in \cite[thm. 6.10]{Jech2006SetTheory} and \cite[thm. 6.8.6.3]{Mimram2020ProgramEqualsProof}. It is also called \enquote{noetherian induction} in \cite[prop. III.6.7]{Bourbaki2004TheoryOfSets}.

  \item In the special case where \( \synp \) is membership in the set \( \BbbN \) of \hyperref[def:natural_numbers]{natural numbers}, we obtain \fullref{thm:strong_induction_on_natural_numbers}. Ironically, it is weaker than \fullref{thm:induction_on_natural_numbers} --- see \cref{thm:strong_induction_implies_induction}.

  Another useful special case is \fullref{thm:induction_on_rooted_trees}.

  We discuss induction more generally in \cref{con:induction}.
\end{comments}
\begin{proof}
  We will assume the antecedent of \eqref{eq:thm:well_founded_induction}:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:thm:well_founded_induction/proof/antecedent}
    \qforall \synx ((\qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \prec \synx \synimplies \synp \syny)) \synimplies \synp \synx).
  \end{equation}

  We will also assume the negation \( \synneg \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synp \synx \) of the consequent of \eqref{eq:thm:well_founded_induction}. With this, we can construct the following proof tree:
  \footnotesize
  \begin{equation*}
    \begin{prooftree}[separation=0.9em]
      \hypo{ [\eqref{eq:thm:well_founded_induction/condition}]^u }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/elim}]{ \synanon }
      \hypo{ [\synneg \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synp \synx]^v }
      \ellipsis {} { \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synneg \synp \synx }

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/imp/elim}]{ \qexists {\synm^{\syn\iota}} (\synneg \synp \synm \synwedge \synneg \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synneg \synp \synx \synwedge \synx \prec \synm)) }

      \hypo{ [\synanon]^t }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_right}]{ \synneg \qexists {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synneg \synp \synx \synwedge \synx \prec \synm) }
      \ellipsis {} { \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} (\synp \synx \synvee \synneg (\synx \prec \synm)) }
      \ellipsis {} { \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} ((\synx \prec \synm) \synimplies \synp \synx) }
      \ellipsis {} { \qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} ((\syny \prec \synm) \synimplies \synp \syny) }

      \hypo{ [\eqref{eq:thm:well_founded_induction/proof/antecedent}]^w }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/elim}]{ \synanon }

      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/imp/elim}]{ \synp \synm }

      \hypo{ [\synanon]^t }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/and/elim_left}]{ \synneg \synp \synm }
      \infer2[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/neg/elim}]{ \synbot }
      \infer[left label={\( t \)}]2[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/exists/elim}]{ \synbot }
      \infer[left label=\( v \)]1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/bot/raa}]{ \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synp \synx }
      \infer[left label=\( w \)]1[\ref{inf:def:propositional_natural_deduction/imp/intro}]{ \synanon }
      \infer1[\ref{inf:def:hol_natural_deduction/forall/intro}]{ \eqref{eq:thm:well_founded_induction} }
    \end{prooftree}
  \end{equation*}
  \normalsize

  The ellipses are rule applications based on \fullref{alg:fol_formula_dualization} and similar principles which we have not formally stated as inference rules for higher-order logic.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Epsilon induction]\label{thm:epsilon_induction}
  The following second-order induction principle can be derived in \hyperref[def:zfc]{\( \logic{ZFC}_2 \)}, or any \hyperref[def:axiomatic_set_theory]{axiomatic set theory} with the \hyperref[def:zfc/foundation]{axiom of foundation}:
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:thm:epsilon_induction/sol}
    \qforall {\synp^{\syn\iota \synimplies \syn\omicron}} \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}}
    \parens[\big]
      {
        \overbrace
          {
            \underbrace{ (\qforall {\syny^{\syn\iota}} (\syny \synin \synx \synimplies \synp \syny)) }_{\mathclap{\substack{\T{inductive} \\ \T{hypothesis}}}}
            \synimplies
            \underbrace{ \synp \synx }_{\mathclap{\substack{\T{inductive step} \\ \T{conclusion}}}}
          }^{\T{inductive step}}
      }
    \synimplies
    \underbrace{ \qforall {\synx^{\syn\iota}} \synp \synx }_{\T{conclusion}}.
  \end{equation}

  Informally, it can be restated as follows:
  \begin{displayquote}
    In order for \( p(x) \) to hold \emph{for every} set \( x \), it is sufficient for \( p(x) \) to hold \emph{whenever} \( p(y) \) holds for every member \( y \) of \( x \).
  \end{displayquote}

  Its corresponding \hyperref[def:fol_adaptation_of_monadic_sol_theory]{first-order adaptation} is
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:thm:epsilon_induction}
    \qforall \synx
    \parens[\big]
      {
        \overbrace
          {
            \underbrace{ (\qforall {\syny \synin \synx} \syn\varphi[\synx \synsubst \syny]) }_{\mathclap{\substack{\T{inductive} \\ \T{hypothesis}}}}
            \synimplies
            \underbrace{ \syn\varphi }_{\mathclap{\substack{\T{inductive step} \\ \T{conclusion}}}}
          }^{\T{inductive step}}
      }
    \synimplies
    \underbrace{ \qforall \synx \syn\varphi }_{\T{conclusion}}.
  \end{equation}

  This is a formula schema in the sense of \cref{def:fol_formula_schema}. We require the \hyperref[con:schemas_and_instances]{instance} of \( \syny \) not to be free in \( \syn\varphi \), but otherwise put no restrictions on the placeholders. Note that we must take the \hyperref[def:fol_quantifier_closure]{universal closure} of each concrete axiom of the schema.
\end{theorem}
\begin{comments}
  \item This induction schema is called \enquote{\( \varepsilon \)-induction} because the set membership symbol \( \in \) is derived from \( \varepsilon \) as explained in \cref{rem:epsilon_and_set_membership}.
\end{comments}
\begin{proof}
  Special case of \fullref{thm:well_founded_induction} with set membership as a well-founded relation.
\end{proof}
